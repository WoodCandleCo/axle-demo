Live map — four layouts

Reworked after three gaps in the current build: no zoom or pan, no way to filter to one person, and no search. Each option below fixes all three; they differ in what the screen is fundamentally organized around.

Shared by all four (non-negotiable in the rework): real pannable/zoomable map with fit-to-fleet and locate controls · search that matches a person or a unit ("Diego", "U-104") · filter down to one person · a last-updated stamp on every vehicle, because a GPS point without a timestamp is a guess · provider attribution once a real basemap lands. The basemap itself is a separate decision — recommendation is MapLibre with open vector tiles (free, no key, no per-load meter, real vector zoom); Mapbox or Google only if a live traffic layer becomes a must-have.

A · Map-first — the map is the page

Full-bleed map with everything floating over it: search and filters on a translucent bar, the vehicle list as a collapsible card in the corner. Feels like the map apps everyone already uses. Best when the question is "show me the yard" and the answer is spatial.

Trade-off: the biggest map and the most familiar interaction, but the vehicle list is a panel you open rather than a table you scan — worse when you want to compare eight crews at once.

B · People-first — the roster drives the map

The left rail becomes a searchable roster of people, not units: photo initial, name, the truck they're in, and how long ago they reported. Type "Diego", click, the map flies to him. Answers the question the office actually asks out loud.

Trade-off: the most direct path to "where's Diego," and riders show under their driver — but a truck with nobody assigned needs its own row, so the roster isn't purely people.

C · Command view — map on top, work table below

A shorter map for context, then a full-width sortable table of every active trip: driver, unit, status, speed, location, job progress, ETA, last update. The table is the work surface; the map answers "where is that." Uses the same table kit as every other screen — sort, filter, search, paging.

Trade-off: the best density and the most consistent with the rest of Axle, but the map shrinks — fine on a desktop, cramped on a phone.

D · Focus mode — one crew at a time

Crews list on the left; picking one takes the map over with just that crew's route, its stops, and a timeline of the day — punched in, left the shop, arrived at each site. Turns the map into the answer to "what happened with this crew today."

Trade-off: by far the best drill-down and it previews the phase-2 trip replay — but the fleet-wide glance takes a second click, so it works best combined with A or C rather than alone.
